== Quality Requirements

=== Quality Tree

Breaking the four quality goals from <<section-introduction-and-goals>> down into the
concrete properties they actually mean for this system:

[plantuml, target=quality_tree, format=png]
....
@startmindmap
* Atlas quality goals
** Functional suitability
*** Move generation never produces\nan illegal move
*** Move generation never omits\na legal move
*** Every game-ending condition is\ndetected correctly (mate, stalemate,\n50-move, insufficient material,\nthreefold repetition)
*** Book moves are always\nre-validated as legal
** Performance efficiency
*** Engine memory stays bounded\nregardless of game length
*** Whole stack fits the\n1 GB e2-micro VM budget
*** Deeper/slower search modes\n(TimeBudget) never exceed\ntheir own deadline
** Maintainability
*** Engine has zero external\ndependencies
*** Engine has no knowledge of\nHTTP/JSON/persistence
*** Web layer never duplicates\nrules the engine already owns
** Reliability
*** Server is the only source\nof truth for legality
*** Frontend never accepts a move\nthe server didn't confirm
*** A server restart's data loss\nis an accepted, documented\ntrade-off, not a silent gap
@endmindmap
....

=== Quality Scenarios

[cols="1,1,3,2", options="header"]
|===
| # | Quality Goal | Scenario | Verification

| QS-1
| Functional suitability
| Move generation is run from the standard starting position to depths 1 through 5.
| `perft_matches_known_values_for_starting_position` asserts the exact node counts from
the Chess Programming Wiki reference (20, 400, 8,902, 197,281, 4,865,609) — any illegal or
missing move at any depth would change these counts.

| QS-2
| Functional suitability
| A position is set up where a move exists that immediately checkmates the opponent.
| `finds_mate_in_one` asserts `find_best_move` returns exactly that move at depth 1.

| QS-3
| Functional suitability
| A position is set up with no legal moves and the side to move not in check.
| `recognizes_stalemate_as_a_draw` asserts the legal-move list is empty, the side to move
is not in check, `find_best_move` returns `None`, and `negamax` scores the position `0`.

| QS-4
| Performance efficiency
| A game is played long enough, or searched deep enough, to fill the transposition table
past its fixed capacity.
| The table can never grow past `TT_SIZE` entries (~1.5 MB) — `tt_store`'s replacement
policy always fits a new entry into an existing slot rather than allocating more, verified
by `tt_store_overwrites_on_index_collision_regardless_of_depth` and
`tt_store_keeps_deeper_entry_on_same_key`.

| QS-5
| Performance efficiency
| The bot is asked to move under `SearchLimit::TimeBudget` (the "max difficulty" mode).
| Iterative deepening in `find_best_move` checks the deadline every `NODE_CHECK_INTERVAL`
(2048) nodes and keeps only the last *completed* depth's result, so a search that runs out
of time mid-depth still returns a legal move rather than blocking past its budget.

| QS-6
| Maintainability
| A change is needed to the search or evaluation logic.
| It can be made, and its unit tests run, entirely within `board_backend` — confirmed by
`board_backend`'s empty `[dependencies]` list, which means no web/serialization crate ever
needs to be touched or recompiled for an engine-only change.

| QS-7
| Reliability
| A client attempts a move that isn't legal in the current position.
| `apply_move_str` validates against `Board::all_legal_moves()` before mutating any state
and returns an error the API turns into `400 Bad Request`; the frontend never applies such
a move to the live game (only an optimistic preview, rolled back on rejection).
|===
